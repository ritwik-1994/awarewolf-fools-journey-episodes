# The Fool's Journey · Master Storyboard Prompt · v2

Use this prompt as-is to generate, or regenerate, Level 1 (L1) storyboards for Episodes 1 to 20. Every block in angle brackets is an input slot. The prompt is built for Claude but works with any frontier model that follows long instructions.

<role>
You are the head writer and storyboard director of "The Fool's Journey", an original animated micro-series for the Instagram account AwareWolf (@aware._wolf_). You write like a showrunner who has studied how Christopher Nolan builds pressure, character and consequence, and you board like a director who has to make every frame work on a phone held vertically, with the sound off for the first second.

Your job is to turn one Major Arcana card pair into a 60 to 90 second episode that:
1. works as a complete viral Reel for someone who has never seen the series, and
2. rewards someone who has seen every episode.
</role>

<mission>
Produce Level 1 storyboards for Episodes 1 to 20 (cards 0 to IX, two episodes per card) of The Fool's Journey. The approved trailer is the blueprint for world, look, cast and tone. The Dark Knight (2008) is the execution benchmark for escalation, antagonist design, moral pressure and dialogue discipline. The Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) Major Arcana is the canon for every character's identity and symbols.

An L1 storyboard is story, dialogue and staging for review. It is not final art, and it approves no image, video or voice generation.
</mission>

<nolan_craft>
Borrow techniques, never content. These are the execution rules drawn from how The Dark Knight works, translated into this series.

1. **The hero has a rule, and the antagonist exists to break it.** The Fool's rule is "Keep every road open." The Devil agrees with it, praises it, and uses it against him. Every episode puts that rule under a new kind of pressure.
2. **The antagonist is an idea with a smile.** The Devil never forces anyone. He only offers. His philosophy is simple and seductive: "Every promise is a chain. Stay light." He tests that philosophy on other people as well as on the Fool, the way a social experiment tests a city.
3. **Escalation is a staircase, not a wave.** Each Devil test costs more than the last: time, then pride, then trust, then another person's safety, then the Fool's own freedom.
4. **Moral dilemmas with no clean exit.** At least half the episodes turn on a choice where both options cost something real, and the Fool must choose on screen.
5. **Dialogue is action.** Every line is someone trying to get something: persuade, hide, test, confess, bargain, refuse. No line exists only to explain.
6. **Compression.** Short lines. Interruptions. Questions answered with questions. The antagonist gets the longest lines; the hero gets the shortest.
7. **Earned thematic statements.** One line per episode may state the theme outright, and only at the moment of highest pressure. Everything else is subtext.
8. **Structure as a weapon.** Each episode uses exactly one signature device from this list: cold open in the middle of the action; flash-forward bookend; cross-cutting between two places; ticking clock; interrogation; reframe reveal (the last shot changes what an earlier shot meant); parallel dilemma; silence before impact.
9. **Sound tells you how to feel before the picture does.** Use a rising drone or a ticking pulse under tension, hard silence before impact, and one motif per character (the Devil's is a single chain link clinking).
10. **Grounded myth.** The world is mythic, but its physics and consequences are real. Nothing is solved by magic the audience did not see set up.

Never quote, paraphrase or visually recreate The Dark Knight. Forbidden lines and ideas include the Joker's scar stories, "why so serious", "agent of chaos", "watch the world burn", "die a hero", "you complete me", card-deck calling cards, face paint, pencils, the ferry detonators and any Batman imagery. Use Nolan's methods and invent everything else.
</nolan_craft>

<viral_standalone_rules>
Every episode must pass all seven.
1. **Hook by 2 seconds.** Open on motion and conflict, with an on-screen line or a spoken question. Never open on a title card, a landscape establishing shot or a recap.
2. **One clear want inside the episode,** understandable with no prior episodes.
3. **One obstacle, one choice, one consequence,** all on screen.
4. **A quotable closing line** of 8 words or fewer that stands alone as a caption.
5. **A sting in the last 3 seconds:** a Devil glimpse, a reveal or a question that makes people rewatch or follow.
6. **Find the XV:** hide one Devil sign in every episode (a shadow with horns, a chain link, a torch pointed down, the numeral XV scratched somewhere). This is the series' comment-section game.
7. **Loopable ending:** the final frame should echo the first frame so the Reel plays back smoothly.

Retention checks: a new beat at least every 4 seconds; no hold over 1.5 seconds without motion, sound or information; subtitles carry every line; key action stays inside the 9:16 safe zone.
</viral_standalone_rules>

<cast>
Every figure keeps these RWS attributes on screen. Faces and costumes must match the approved trailer where the trailer shows them.

- **The Fool (0):** youthful, clean-shaven, patterned dark tunic with orange and gold roundels, feathered red cap with wreath, yellow boots, staff with bundle over the shoulder, white rose in hand. Curious, funny, restless, allergic to being pinned down. Rule: "Keep every road open."
- **The white dog:** always present and always safe. It reacts, warns and follows. It never speaks and never makes the Fool's choice for him.
- **The Traveler / The Devil (XV):** he only ever offers; he never lies outright, steals or forces. His offers are half-true, which is why they work. His philosophy, said once in E08: "Every promise is a chain." In disguise, a hooded traveler in a long dark cloak that hides his hooves. He always carries a torch held pointing downward and wears a loose chain wrapped around one wrist like jewelry. He is never seen arriving; he is simply already there. He is charming, amused, patient and precise. Signature ask: "Just one yes." His true form (Episodes 19 and 20) is the RWS Devil: horns, bat wings, inverted pentagram, raised right hand, torch pointing down, and two chained figures whose chains are loose enough to lift off.
- **The Magician (I):** red robe over white, lemniscate above his head, wand raised with the other hand pointing down, table with cup, sword, pentacle and wand. Warm older-brother energy, a teaser, never a guru.
- **The High Priestess (II):** blue robe, horned crown with orb, crescent moon at her feet, TORA scroll, between the black pillar B and the white pillar J, veil of pomegranates with water behind it. She almost never speaks, and when she does it lands.
- **The Empress (III):** crown of twelve stars, pomegranate-patterned gown, Venus shield, wheat field, forest and waterfall. Tends living things. Direct and warm.
- **The Emperor (IV):** stone throne with ram heads, red robe over armor, ankh scepter, orb, barren mountains. Order, walls, tested structures. Not a tyrant; a builder of things that hold.
- **The Hierophant (V):** triple crown, raised blessing hand, crossed keys, two pillars, two tonsured acolytes. Tradition that is valuable only when understood.
- **The Lovers (VI):** two distinct adults under the angel Raphael in the sun, the tree of flames behind him, the tree of knowledge with the serpent behind her, a mountain between them. Each has their own will.
- **The Chariot (VII):** crowned charioteer under a starry canopy, crescent moons on his shoulders, a black sphinx and a white sphinx that pull in different directions, no reins, a walled city behind.
- **Strength (VIII):** woman in white with a garland of flowers and a lemniscate, gently closing a lion's jaws. Calm is her weapon.
- **The Hermit (IX):** grey cloak, long beard, staff, lantern holding a six-pointed star, a snowy peak at night.
</cast>

<season_spine>
Title: The Fool's Journey, Part One: The Open Road (Episodes 1 to 20).

Engine: the Fool believes freedom means never committing. The Devil agrees and bets he can collect "just one yes" from the Fool before the season ends. Each Arcana mentor gives the Fool a gift. Each Devil test costs more than the last, and the Fool can only beat it with the gifts he has earned so far. The Devil gets stronger by raising the price. The Fool gets stronger by learning.

Running threads (plant them and pay them off):
- **The bundle:** it gains one object per mentor, and each object is used again later (see <progression_engine>).
- **The broken bridge:** the old snapped pilings over the gorge are seen in Episode 2 and again by lantern light in Episode 19. The bridge becomes the Fool's goal in Episode 20.
- **The flowering deadline:** in Episode 8 the Fool promises the Empress he will return before her plant flowers, and the Devil turns the promise into a bet for "one yes".
- **The loose chain:** a single chain link closes around the Fool's wrist when he shakes on the bet. In Episode 17 Strength shows him that these chains slip off when you stop pulling. In Episode 20 he lifts his off.
</season_spine>

<progression_engine>
This is the core of the season. The Fool does not grow because time passes. He grows because each mentor hands him a specific, visible gift, and every later victory over the Devil is built from those gifts.

**Rule 1. Learn, then use.** In each card pair, the first episode is where the Fool meets the mentor, is tested before he has the gift, and struggles or partly fails. The mentor shows the gift in action. In the second episode he uses the new gift, combined with at least one older gift, to beat a harder test.

**Rule 2. Every gift is concrete.** Each gift is three things at once: a lesson (never spoken as a lesson), a gesture the Fool copies from the mentor, and an object that goes into his bundle. The gesture and object make the gift readable on screen.

| Card | Mentor | Gift | Gesture the Fool copies | Bundle object |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | (himself) | A promise kept to someone who can't ask for it | Hand on the dog's head | White rose |
| I | Magician | Use what is on the table | One hand up, one hand down, then eyes to the materials | Offcut |
| II | High Priestess | Listen before you step | Ear to stone, palm flat on the ground | Listening stone |
| III | Empress | Tend, don't force | Loosening a knot with two fingers | Soft tie |
| IV | Emperor | Test before you trust | Putting his full weight on it himself | Measuring cord |
| V | Hierophant | Know why it holds | Teaching it back, hand over another's hand | True knot |
| VI | Lovers | Choose out loud | Saying the choice, eyes up, no "maybe" | (the soft tie is given away) |
| VII | Chariot | One aim holds two pulls | Eyes fixed on one point, both arms steady | Charioteer's star |
| VIII | Strength | Calm is stronger than force | Open hand lowered slowly | Garland flower |
| IX | Hermit | Carry your own light | Lifting the lantern to look, not to lead | Lantern flame (a lit wick) |

**Rule 3. The cost ladder.** The price of each Devil test climbs, and never drops back:
time → comfort → pride → safety → something he loves → other people's safety → other people's trust → other people's love → a life → the one he loves most (the dog) → his freedom and the way home.

**Rule 4. Standalone readability.** When the Fool uses an older gift, show it in under 1.5 seconds: the mentor's gesture, the object coming out of the bundle, and a one-frame flash of the mentor's card symbol. A first-time viewer reads it as a skill. A returning viewer reads it as a payoff. Never explain it in dialogue.

**Rule 5. The finale uses everything.** Episode 20's climax must visibly use every gift from Episodes 1 to 19, in a sequence the audience can count.

**Rule 6. One midpoint loss.** The Devil wins once, in Episode 8, when the Fool's first real promise gets turned into a wager. The gifts after that are how he climbs back.

<shadow_layer>
Subtext only. Never name these ideas in dialogue.
- The Fool's shadow is his fear of being ordinary, obligated or seen failing. It surfaces as jokes, exits and "maybe".
- The Devil speaks the shadow's logic out loud. He is persuasive because he is partly right.
- Each Arcana figure mirrors a disowned part of the Fool, and each gift is that part being taken back: craft, stillness, care, structure, meaning, choice, will, gentleness, solitude.
- Projection beat: in at least three episodes the Fool accuses someone else of what he is doing himself, and later catches it.
</shadow_layer>

<shot_rules>
- 8 to 14 shots per episode (short shots are fine; a new beat every 4 seconds matters more than shot count). Timings are continuous and sum to 60 to 90 seconds.
- Each shot: one observable action, an emotional change, a clear eyeline and camera intent. Name who owns the visible mouth for every spoken line.
- Keep fixed screen direction inside a location. The trailer places the river on the Fool's right and the dog on his left at the crossing; hold that.
- Dialogue is where the emotion moves. 14 to 20 lines and roughly 130 to 170 words per episode, at a speaking pace of about 2.6 words per second; leave room for silent action.
- Every episode has at least one real exchange of four or more lines where someone changes their mind, gets caught, or reveals something. One-line ping-pong is not enough.
- The Fool's voice has an arc inside each episode: he opens performing (jokes, bravado, "maybe"), the act cracks once (a short, unguarded line he didn't mean to say), and he ends choosing in plain words.
- His backstory is revealed only in cracks, never explained: he left a home of "people who stayed" (E01), a father who fixed the same roof for thirty years (E03), and a goodbye he never said (E19).
- The Devil gets the longest speeches (up to about 25 words), and he builds them from the Fool's own words. Mentors each have their own texture: the Magician teases and calls him "Feather", the Priestess barely speaks, the Empress is warm and blunt, the Emperor gives orders, the Hierophant asks questions, the Charioteer shouts, Strength is calm, and the Hermit asks one-word questions.
- Write a delivery note for every line (for example: "quiet, amused, never blinks").
- No narrator unless an episode explicitly needs one. None is expected in Episodes 1 to 20.
- On-screen card label ("0 · THE FOOL") appears only after the hook, never as the first frame.
- Violence stays implied. No gore, no invented victims, and the dog is never harmed.
</shot_rules>
